changed, no data was bought, nothing was executed.

**Headline: PBO across all 12 hypotheses is 14.8% (roll-adjusted) — low. But that number is carried entirely

## 1. Exact methodology

Implemented from Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, *Journal of Computational Finance* **20**(4), 39–69
(2017), as summarised in `QUANTITATIVE_RESEARCH_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md`. Code: `nq_bot/pbo.py`,
driver `run_pbo.py`, tests `tests/test_pbo.py`.

1. Build a **T × N matrix** of per-session P&L in dollars for one MNQ contract: T sessions, N strategies.
2. Split the T rows into **S contiguous, equal blocks in time order**. S must be even.
3. Form **every balanced combination** of S/2 blocks as the training set J; its complement J̄ is the test set.
   S = 16 gives C(16,8) = **12,870** splits.
4. For each split: compute each strategy's Sharpe on J, take the argmax as the in-sample winner n\*, then find
   n\*'s **rank** among all N strategies' Sharpes on J̄ (1 = worst, N = best; ties take the average rank).
5. Relative rank **ω = rank / (N+1)**; logit **λ = ln(ω / (1−ω))**.
6. **PBO = fraction of splits with λ ≤ 0**, i.e. where the in-sample winner landed below the out-of-sample
   median.

Sharpe is mean ÷ standard deviation of daily P&L, **not annualised**. Annualising multiplies every strategy
by the same constant and so cannot change a rank, which is all CSCV uses.

**Implementation note.** Sharpes for all 12,870 splits are computed from per-block aggregates (count, sum,
sum of squares) via one matrix multiply, not by re-slicing the data 12,870 times. This is an exact
reformulation, not an approximation.

### Validation against known answers
`tests/test_pbo.py` checks the implementation on three cases whose answer is known in advance:

| Case | Expected | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| 12 pure-noise strategies | ~50% | **50.1%** |
| One strategy with a genuine persistent edge | ~0% | **0.0%** |
| One strategy great in the first half, bad in the second | high | **70.0%** |

**ORB uses the full-sample run, not the walk-forward OOS.** The walk-forward series begins 2020-05-13 and so

which must not be zero-filled. The full-sample ORB run spans 2019-05-14 → 2025-06-30. This is a deliberate
choice and it means the ORB column here is the *more favourable* of the two ORB results.

### Zero-filling: what was and was not done
A session on which a strategy held no position has P&L of **exactly zero**. That is a real observation and is
filled with 0 deliberately. **No missing observation was invented and no unfavourable observation was
removed.** A strategy that could not be evaluated over part of the calendar is a different case and would
have to be excluded rather than zero-filled — that is precisely why ORB's walk-forward series was not used.

### The subgroup result, which matters more than the headline

Comparing a 48-active-day strategy against a 1,520-active-day one on daily Sharpe is not like-for-like, so the
set was split by trading frequency (threshold: 200 active days, fixed before looking at the outcome).

| Group | N | PBO | Winner's Sharpe IS → OOS |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | 12 | 14.8% | +0.0649 → **+0.0319** |
| **Intraday / high-frequency** (ORB, H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, H7, H11) | 8 | **60.8%** | +0.0252 → **−0.0370** |
| Event / low-frequency (H6, H8, H9, H10) | 4 | 25.9% | +0.0644 → **+0.0383** |

**Among the eight intraday strategies, picking the in-sample best gives a strategy that lands below the
out-of-sample median 61% of the time and has a negative out-of-sample Sharpe on average.** That is textbook
backtest overfitting, measured rather than suspected. It is independent confirmation that closing intraday
research was correct — and it is the first quantitative evidence of *why*, beyond "eleven of them failed."

The event group's 25.9% is elevated but not damning. Within it, daily Sharpe over the training period:

| H6 turn of month | $2,714 | 73 | +0.0108 |

### Sensitivity to the number of blocks

| S | Splits | Block size | PBO as published | PBO roll-adjusted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 70 | 191 | 8.6% | 11.4% |
| 10 | 252 | 153 | 10.7% | 12.7% |
| 12 | 924 | 127 | **18.8%** | **21.5%** |
| 14 | 3,432 | 109 | 13.4% | 16.7% |
| 16 | 12,870 | 95 | 10.6% | 14.8% |

**Limitations of this sensitivity check.** The estimates are not monotone in S — S = 12 is the high outlier in
both columns. This is block-boundary sensitivity: where the cuts fall relative to market regimes changes which
splits are formed, and with only 1,520 rows the blocks are short. The honest statement is **PBO is somewhere
in the 9–22% range depending on partitioning**, not that it is exactly 14.8%. S = 16 is reported as the
headline because it gives the most splits, not because it is the most correct.

## 4. Data-quality problems found

1. **Session count: 1,535, not 1,588.** The project brief said 1,588 common sessions. Both numbers are
   right and count different things: 1,588 sessions have ≥1 regular-hours bar (the definition
   `RESULTS_ORB.md` used), while 1,535 have ≥300 (the definition `family3.Session` uses and every Family 3/4
   result relies on). The 53-session difference is **half-days** — Memorial Day, July 4th, Labor Day,
   Thanksgiving, MLK, Presidents' Day — carrying 185–210 bars each. After block trimming, 1,520 rows were
   used and **15 were dropped**, reported in `runs/pbo.json`.
2. **The family2 per-trade files had been lost** to `.gitignore`. Regenerated and verified against the
   published reports.
3. **Multi-day P&L attribution.** Each trade's P&L is booked to its **exit** day, the day it is realised.
   For H6, which holds about five sessions, that makes the daily series spikier than a mark-to-market series
   would be and slightly understates its daily Sharpe. Trade-level data is all that exists, so this is
   recorded as a limitation rather than smoothed over.
4. **Roll contamination** was carried into the second matrix via `nq_bot.rolls`, whose detector is a
   heuristic (`AUDIT_2026_11_ROLLS.md`). The roll-adjusted PBO is therefore approximate in the same way the
   roll-adjusted t-statistics are.

## 5. Does this change the project's research conclusions?

**For the intraday family: yes, it strengthens the existing conclusion with direct evidence.** PBO of 60.8%
with a negative out-of-sample Sharpe says selection among those eight was worse than useless. Closing intraday
research was right, and now there is a measurement rather than an inference behind it.

**For H8: no — and the reason is the most important thing in this document.** H8 was the in-sample winner in
8,666 of 12,870 splits and generalised above the out-of-sample median in most of them. A naïve reading is
"PBO is low, so H8 is fine." That reading is wrong, because **PBO is computed entirely within the training
period (2019-05 → 2025-06). It cannot see the holdout.** And the holdout failed, at t 0.34 (0.25
roll-adjusted) on 11 events.

Those two facts are consistent, not contradictory, and the distinction is worth stating precisely:

- **PBO** asks: *does the in-sample winner generalise across random splits of this period?* For H8, yes.
- **The holdout** asks: *does it still work in a later period?* For H8, no.

An effect that was real during 2019–2025 and has since decayed passes the first test and fails the second.
That is exactly the pattern the published literature on this effect describes, and it is why P1's own authors
argue a hold-out alone is unreliable — the converse is equally true, and **PBO alone is unreliable too.**

**So H8's status is unchanged by this experiment:** unconfirmed, below its original bar on roll-adjusted data,
sitting on the 5% multiple-testing line, with its only clean out-of-sample test failed. PBO adds one piece of
good news — the selection procedure that found it was not obviously picking noise — and removes nothing.

### What this experiment does not show
- It is **not a profitability test.** A low PBO does not mean any strategy has an edge.
- It does not validate H8, H10, or any strategy.
- It does not address data integrity; `nq_bot/pbo.py` would return the same answer on contaminated prices.
  Data integrity and statistical integrity are separate problems, and this experiment addresses only the
  second.
- The procedure CSCV models — "pick the best of N" — is **not exactly the procedure this project ran**, which
  was "test each hypothesis against a fixed pre-registered bar." PBO answers the closely related question of
  whether selecting the apparent winner would have generalised, which is the right question to ask about H8,
  but the mapping is not perfect and should not be presented as though it were.

## 6. Reproducing this

```
py run_pbo.py                  # writes runs/pbo.json
py -m pytest tests/test_pbo.py -q
```
Deterministic: no randomness anywhere in CSCV, and the only seeded randomness is in the tests.
