# Cross-strategy post-mortem: all 12 hypotheses tested on MNQ

Written 2026-11-11, after the strategy search on this data was closed (2026-11-09). Every number below was
**re-derived from the run artifacts in this repository**, not copied from an earlier summary. `runs/family3_results.json`
and `runs/family4_results.json` were missing and were regenerated by re-running the pre-registered scripts
unchanged; all six re-ran to the published figures exactly. The verification table is saved as
`runs/postmortem_verification.json`.

Nothing here explains a negative result by anything other than the numbers. Where a cause cannot be
determined from the data, it says so.

## 0. The one-line finding

**Remove transaction costs entirely and eleven of the twelve hypotheses still fail.** At zero cost their
t-statistics span **−1.59 to +1.76** — the range you would expect from noise. H8 is the only one outside it,
at 3.07. Cheaper execution would not have rescued a single strategy here.

## 1. Shared setup

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | MNQ (Micro Nasdaq-100), $2/point, 0.25 tick |

| Training period | 2019-05-06 → 2025-06-30, 2,167,313 one-minute bars, 1,588 regular sessions |
| Holdout | 2025-07 → 2026-10, not downloaded until H8 passed, then used once (H8 only) |

The rising bar follows Harvey, Liu & Zhu (*RFS* 2016), who argue a new factor should clear t ≈ 3.

## 3. Each hypothesis in detail

### ORB + VWAP/trend filter — the pre-protocol baseline
- **Hypothesis:** price breaking the first 15–30 minutes' range continues in that direction, filtered by EMA/VWAP trend.
- **Exploits:** intraday breakout continuation.
- **Method:** 5 anchored walk-forward folds, 8-combination grid (range 15/30 min × R:R 1.5/2.0 × ATR stop 1.0/1.5), only unseen folds counted. Not pre-registered — it predates the protocol and is the reason the protocol exists.
- **Costs' role:** none. The full-sample run loses **−$4,735 before commissions**; the walk-forward OOS is −$3,423 at zero cost.
- **Why it failed:** every fold lost (−$780, −$424, −$1,165 for folds 2–4), and every year, both sides, both regimes and every entry hour lost money. A strategy that loses uniformly across every partition is not mis-parameterised; the premise is wrong.

### H1 — First half-hour momentum (Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, *JFE* 2018)
- **Hypothesis:** the sign of the previous-close → 10:00 ET return predicts the 15:30 → 16:00 return.
- **Exploits:** published intraday momentum, attributed to late-day informed trading.
- **Method:** 1,534 sessions, one trade/day, long if predictor > 0 else short, time exit at 16:00, stop at 1.5 × median recent 15:30→16:00 move. No tuned parameters, so the whole period is one test.
- **Costs' role:** none. Gross before commissions **−$4,476**; −$3,199 at zero cost.
- **Why it failed:** failed all five checks. The predictor's correlation with the outcome is **0.075**, sign hit-rate **49.2%** — a coin flip. The published effect does not appear in MNQ over this period; the strategy is significantly negative *with* costs (t −2.25).

### H2 — Rest-of-day momentum (Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, *JFE* 2021)
- **Hypothesis:** the previous-close → 15:30 return predicts the last 30 minutes.
- **Exploits:** the same literature, attributed to option-dealer gamma hedging and leveraged-ETF rebalancing.
- **Method:** identical to H1 but with the rest-of-day predictor. Baltussen et al. include NQ and their sample ends May 2020, so June 2020 → June 2025 is genuinely out of sample relative to them.
- **Costs' role:** **decisive in sign, irrelevant to the verdict.** Net −$2,611; at zero cost **+$250**, which is **$0.20 per trade** against $2.24 of friction, and t₀ = 0.09.
- **Why it failed:** correlation 0.066, sign hit-rate 50.6%. A gross edge of 20 cents a trade is indistinguishable from zero, so this is not "a real edge destroyed by costs."

### H3 — Overnight drift 2–3 a.m. ET (Boyarchenko, Larsen & Whelan, *RFS* 2023)
- **Hypothesis:** US index futures rise between 02:00 and 03:00 ET (the European open).
- **Exploits:** a documented liquidity/risk-transfer pattern at the European open.
- **Method:** buy the 02:00 bar open, sell the 03:00 bar open, every weekday; 1,576 trades.
- **Pre-registered expectation:** *fail* — the same authors reported the effect near zero from 2021 (NY Fed, July 2026). **This was a pipeline check, not a profit attempt.**
- **Costs' role:** **decisive in sign.** Gross **+$1,378.50**, net −$576, commissions $1,954. Zero-cost +$2,954, t₀ = 1.69.
- **Why it failed:** as predicted. And it **reproduced the published decay precisely**: mean 02:00→03:00 move **+2.27 points/day before 2021, +0.16 after**. This is the most valuable negative result in the set — it is direct evidence the pipeline measures real effects correctly, which is what licenses believing its failures.

### H4 — Liquidity sweep of the prior day's high/low (retail folklore)
- **Hypothesis:** price running through an obvious level and closing back inside marks exhaustion and reverses.
- **Exploits:** claimed stop-loss clustering beyond prior-day high/low. No peer-reviewed support; this tested the popular claim directly.
- **Method:** sweep ≥ 2 points beyond PDH/PDL between 09:45–15:00, reversal close within 5 bars, stop beyond the sweep extreme, target 2R; first sweep per side per day only. 288 trades.
- **Costs' role:** none — it was **profitable net** (+$1,204, PF 1.20).
- **Why it failed:** t = 0.71 against a bar of 3.0, and only 2 of 6 years positive. Also **asymmetric in a way the hypothesis does not predict**: shorts made +$1,931 (138 trades) while longs lost −$728 (150 trades). A mechanism said to be symmetric that only works on one side is more consistent with the index's upward drift penalising longs than with stop-hunting.

### H5 — VWAP pullback in a trend (retail folklore)
- **Hypothesis:** in an established intraday trend, pullbacks to session VWAP resume the trend.
- **Exploits:** claimed trend continuation from a mean-reversion entry.
- **Method:** existing regime filter (EMA 20/50 alignment, slope, price vs VWAP, volatility), 10:00–14:00 signals, stop beyond VWAP − 0.5 ATR, target 2R. 1,399 trades.
- **Costs' role:** decisive in sign. Net −$2,336; zero-cost +$798 = **$0.57/trade**, t₀ = 0.32.
- **Why it failed:** failed every check, 1 of 6 years positive, and both sides lost (−$1,995 short, −$341 long). Win rate 33.2% with a 2R target is almost exactly break-even by construction, so the strategy carried no edge in either direction.

### H6 — Turn of the month (Lakonishok & Smidt 1988; Xu & McConnell; Carchano & Tornero)
- **Hypothesis:** equities earn abnormal returns on trading days −1 through +3 around month end.
- **Exploits:** the one calendar effect reported to persist in S&P 500 futures.
- **Method:** buy at the 15:59 close on day −2, sell at the 15:59 close on day +3, 5% disaster stop. 73 trades.
- **Costs' role:** none — profitable net (+$2,892); costs are 5% of the result.
- **Why it failed:** t = 0.45, and it **did not beat the random-long-hold benchmark**. This is the clearest instance of the set's dominant confound: a long-only overnight hold in a rising market makes money without any effect being present.

### H7 — Overnight-gap reversal
- **Hypothesis:** the opening gap reverses toward the previous close.
- **Exploits:** reported overnight/intraday return reversal in NQ futures.
- **Method:** gap z-score ≥ 1.0 and |gap| ≥ 10 points, trade against the gap from the 09:31 open, target = gap fill, time exit 15:55. 439 trades.
- **Costs' role:** none. Loses **−$2,758 at zero cost**.
- **Why it failed:** t −0.74 and negative gross. Over this period gaps continued more often than they filled, i.e. the effect ran opposite to the hypothesis.

### H8 — Pre-FOMC drift (Lucca & Moench, *JF* 2015) — THE SURVIVOR
- **Hypothesis:** equities drift up in the 24 hours before a scheduled FOMC statement.
- **Exploits:** a risk premium paid for holding equity exposure into a scheduled announcement.
- **Method:** buy the 14:00 ET bar open the day before, sell the 13:55 ET bar close on statement day, 3% disaster stop. 48 scheduled statements from Fed calendars; unscheduled 2020 actions excluded.
- **Pre-registered expectation:** *fail* — Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert report it essentially disappeared after 2015.
- **Costs' role:** negligible. $2.24 against a $148 mean; t moves 3.02 → 3.07.
- **Why it passed:** all four checks, including beating the 95th percentile of random same-length long holds ($148 vs $99), which is what distinguishes it from H6/H9/H10.
- **What has happened since:** holdout **t 0.34 on 11 events** (failed); MES replication t 3.56; M2K t 2.45; gold negative control t 0.59 (inconclusive); placebo test on sham dates **clean in all three markets**; Nikkei replication **invalid on data coverage**. Status: *replicated candidate*, decided forward from Dec 8–9, 2026 under `PREREG_H8_FORWARD.md`.

### H9 — Macro announcement-day premium (Savor & Wilson, *JFQA* 2013)
- **Hypothesis:** equities earn higher returns on scheduled CPI/jobs-report days.
- **Method:** buy the 15:59 close the day before a BLS CPI or Employment Situation release, sell the 15:59 close on release day, 3% stop. FOMC days excluded as already tested. 141 trades.
- **Costs' role:** none — profitable net (+$1,247).
- **Why it failed:** t = 0.23 against 3.5, and the mean of **$8.85** on a $2/point contract is about 4.4 index points — within ordinary daily noise. Same long-only drift confound as H6.

### H10 — Pre-holiday effect (Ariel, *JF* 1990; Lakonishok & Smidt 1988)
- **Hypothesis:** equities rise on the session before an exchange holiday.
- **Method:** pre-holiday days derived from the data's own session calendar (no hand-made list, so no cherry-picking); buy prior 15:59 close, sell pre-holiday 15:59 close, 3% stop. 59 trades.
- **Costs' role:** none — profitable net (+$3,876, PF 1.84).
- **Why it failed:** t = 1.70 against 3.5. **The strongest of the eleven failures**, and at zero cost t₀ = 1.76, still well short. With 59 observations and this variance it cannot be resolved on this data; it is not disproven, only unproven.

### H11 — Opening-hour trend after a high-range day
- **Hypothesis:** intraday momentum is stronger after volatile sessions (the volatility condition in Gao et al.).
- **Exploits:** a volatility-regime-conditioned version of H1, with a different window.
- **Method:** previous session's range > 1.5 × median of prior 20; trade the sign of the 09:30→10:29 return from the 10:30 open, stop at the opening-hour extreme, exit 15:55. 253 trades.
- **Costs' role:** none — profitable net (+$1,983).
- **Why it failed:** t = 0.36 against 3.5. Conditioning H1 on volatility moved it from significantly negative to indistinguishable from zero, which is a change in sign but not the appearance of an edge.

## 4. Common causes across the eleven failures

**(a) Three distinct failure modes, not one.**

| Mode | Hypotheses | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| Negative premise | ORB, H1, H7 | lose money even at zero cost; lose across every partition |
| Gross edge smaller than one tick | H2, H3, H5 | positive gross, negative net, zero-cost t ≤ 1.69 |
| Long-only drift mistaken for an effect | H6, H9, H10, H11, H4 | profitable net, t far below bar, no benchmark outperformance |

**(b) The dominant confound is the market's own upward drift.** Five of the eleven failures made money and still
failed. Every one of them is long-biased or long-only. This is why the protocol requires beating a random
same-length long-hold benchmark, and it is exactly what separated H8 (beat it, $148 vs $99) from H6 (did not).
Without that benchmark, four of these eleven failures would have been reported as successes.

**(c) Where a gross edge exists at all, it is smaller than the friction.** H2 $0.20/trade, H5 $0.57, H3 $1.87 —
against $2.24 per round turn. MNQ's round-trip friction is about 1.1 ticks, so the measurable intraday structure
in this data is **smaller than one tick**. No broker or execution improvement closes a gap that starts below the
minimum price increment.

**(d) Published effects decayed before the sample began.** H1, H3 and H8 all have documented post-publication
decay, and the data agrees in each case — most sharply for H3 (+2.27 → +0.16 points/day). The sample period is
mostly *after* these papers published. This is not a flaw in the tests; it is the finding.

**(e) Trade count and significance move in opposite directions here.** The four highest-n strategies
(1,204–1,576 trades) all have negative t. The two highest t-statistics come from the two smallest samples
(H8 n=48, H10 n=59). More observations did not produce more significance, because the high-frequency strategies
have no edge to accumulate while the event strategies cannot accumulate observations fast enough.

## 5. Does 1-minute OHLCV provide enough information to find a persistent edge here?

The honest answer is split, and the split matters.

**The data is sufficient to measure effects, and that is established, not assumed.** H3 reproduced a published
effect *and* its published decay from this exact data. A pipeline that can recover a known result and its known
disappearance is measuring real things. The eleven failures are therefore evidence about the market, not about
the measurement.

**The data is structurally insufficient for one of the five directions tested.** 1-minute OHLCV contains no
bid/ask, no trade direction, no order-book depth, no queue position and no order-flow imbalance. H4 was
therefore a test of a *price-shaped proxy* for stop-hunting, not of stop-hunting itself. Its failure is weak
evidence against the microstructure hypothesis — a genuinely different test would need MBO or MBP-10 data, which
costs orders of magnitude more than the $9–10 per market this project has been spending. **This is the one place
where "insufficient data" is the correct diagnosis rather than "no effect."**

**For the other four directions the data is adequate and the answer is negative.** Intraday momentum (H1, H2,
H11), volatility regimes (H11 plus the regime filter gating H5 and ORB), calendar effects (H6, H10) and
event-driven effects (H8, H9) are all fully expressible in 1-minute OHLCV. They were tested and they failed —
except H8.

**The broader reading.** Eleven of twelve failing is the expected outcome for a retail participant using
1-minute bars on the most liquid equity index futures in existence, against firms with order-book feeds,
colocation and research staff. The project's one survivor is an event premium that pays about **$148 per event,
eight times a year** — roughly $1,015 a year on one contract. The edges that survive here are real-but-tiny,
and tiny is what a retail-capacity participant should expect to be left with: it is precisely the edges too
small for institutional capital to pursue that remain available.

## 6. What evidence would justify further research

| Direction | Justified? | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Statistical arbitrage between the owned markets | **Yes, once** | MNQ/MES/M2K/MGC/NKD minute bars are already paid for, so the test is free. Requires a specific economic hypothesis, pre-registered rules, a holdout, and costs doubled for two legs. This is the only untested direction with no data cost. |
| Order-flow microstructure | Only with new data | Needs MBO/MBP-10. Do not re-test with OHLCV proxies; H4 already did that. Not justified at this project's budget. |
| Longer horizons (multi-day to weekly) | Possibly | The friction argument inverts: $2.24 against a multi-day move is negligible. But this becomes asset allocation rather than trading, and is better served by the retirement-account plan than by this bot. |
| More intraday patterns on this data | **No** | Twelve tested, eleven failed, and the gross edges are below one tick. Another family would mainly add another chance of a false pass. |
| Machine learning on this data | **No** | The protocol's strength is testing few pre-specified hypotheses against a high bar; ML searches a vast space and reports the best fit. For daily-horizon prediction there are ~1,900 independent observations, and for event work 59. `nq_bot/regime.py` already encodes the right constraint: *"An ML classifier can replace this later ONLY if it beats it out-of-sample."* That condition is not currently met by any candidate because none exists. |
| Re-optimising any of the eleven failures | **Never on this data** | Prohibited by `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`. Three lose money at zero cost; five are the market's drift. Tuning them would manufacture a backtest, not find an edge. |

## 7. What this post-mortem does not claim

- It does not claim the eleven hypotheses are false in general — only that they do not clear their pre-registered
  bars on MNQ, 2019-05 to 2025-06, after realistic costs. H10 in particular is unproven, not disproven.
- It does not claim H8 is profitable. H8 cleared a training bar and has since failed its only clean
  out-of-sample test. Its status is a candidate under forward test.
- It does not claim the data is the limiting factor except for order-flow microstructure, where it is.
- It offers no explanation for a negative result beyond what the numbers support. Where the cause is not
  determinable from the data — for example *why* gaps continued rather than filled over this period — this
  document says nothing rather than speculating.
